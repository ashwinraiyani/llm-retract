I would not make primary data collection a general public-opinion survey. It should directly feed your proposed Exposure–Sensitivity–Adaptive Capacity → HHRI framework.

Your proposal already defines these three dimensions.

A. Exposure

Collect:

- Daily outdoor exposure duration
- Workplace temperature perception
- Working hours during peak heat
- Availability of shade
- Availability of drinking water
- Access to cooling
- Housing heat exposure
- Local environmental conditions
- Frequency of heatwave exposure

B. Sensitivity

Collect:

- Age group
- Household size
- Occupation
- Outdoor/indoor occupation
- Existing health vulnerability, using appropriate non-identifying categories
- Previous heat-related illness
- Heat-stress symptoms
- Disability-related vulnerability where relevant
- Socio-economic characteristics

C. Adaptive Capacity

Collect:

- Access to healthcare
- Distance/accessibility to healthcare
- Access to drinking water
- Cooling arrangements
- Electricity availability
- Awareness of heat alerts
- Awareness of Heat Action Plan messages
- Ability to modify working hours
- Access to shaded/cooling spaces
- Emergency-response awareness

These variables can then be converted into indicators for the HHRI, complementing the secondary datasets described in your proposal.

Suggested primary-data sample design

For an ₹42.60 lakh, 18-month project, I would propose something such as:

| Component                                         | Suggested sample |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| General households                                | 1,500            |
| Outdoor workers                                   | 750              |
| Elderly/vulnerable respondents                    | 300              |
| Healthcare professionals/facilities               | 150              |
| Local-government/disaster-management stakeholders | 100              |
| Total primary respondents                         | ~2,800           |

The final sample should be determined by your statistical design and the number of selected districts, rather than simply maximizing the number of respondents.

Suggested geographic structure

Rather than surveying all Gujarat districts, you could select approximately 10–12 districts representing different heat-risk environments, consistent with your proposal's M&E target of 10+ districts.

For example, sampling could cover:

- Highly urbanized/high UHI areas
- Semi-arid areas
- Coastal areas
- Industrial areas
- Agricultural/outdoor-worker dominated areas
- Higher and lower healthcare-access areas
